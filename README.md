# Explorer

World of Warcraft: Forever addon. Puts a pin on the world map for every area you still have to visit for the Legacy "Explore" challenges (Legacy Challenges > Adventure > Explorer).

## What it does

- **The zone you're in:** a spyglass pin on each area the challenge hasn't ticked yet. Hover it for the area name and the zone's progress. Click it to set a waypoint there. On the continent map they show as small gold dots.
- **Other zones:** open a zone's map and its unexplored areas show as small faint markers that stay out of the way of the map's other icons. Hover one for its name, click it for a waypoint. "Zones I'm not in" in the settings switches them to full pins or off.
- **Continent maps:** hover a zone and a line under its name says "Explored 7/9". Fully explored zones have no line. Only the zone you're in has markers at this level.
- **Progress:** each area's state is the challenge's tick, or the character's own map where the challenge says nothing. `/ep check` prints what the game reports for a zone, area by area.
- **Route:** the areas you have left, in an order worth walking. Your zone's areas come first, as a short path from where you stand, measured by the ground and the roads; then the other zones, each time the one quickest to reach (flight paths you know, boats, zeppelins, the tram). It rides what your faction can: its own flight masters, boats and zeppelins and the ones open to both, and nothing that starts or ends inside a capital of the other faction. Where that gets nowhere it takes the other faction's boats as a last resort, as few as will do, and says whose they are: for the Horde, the boat from Auberdine and the portal at Rut'theran Village, the only way to Teldrassil. "Use the other faction's boats where nothing else goes" in the settings turns that off; the zone then comes last, marked as having no way there. Pins are numbered in that order and joined by a line. "Route covers" in the settings picks your zone, your continent or everything, and zones more than 5 levels above you are left out unless you say otherwise.
- **Arrow:** points at the next stop and turns as you turn, with the stop's name, distance and time. Where the straight line is blocked it points along the road. For a stop in another zone it points at the first leg of the trip ("Talk to the Flightmaster to travel to ..."). Click it for a waypoint, right-click to skip the stop, drag to move, scroll to resize.
- **Popup** when an area gets its tick: a banner near the top of the screen with the area, its zone and the zone's new count. `/ep test` (or "Sample" in the settings) shows a sample you can drag to move, scroll on to resize, and right-click to reset.
- **Chat line** for the same, if you want it in the log too.
- `/explorer` (or `/ep`) opens the window: progress per zone (click a zone to open its map) and the settings.
- `/ep next` sets a waypoint on the nearest unexplored area of the zone you're in.
- `/ep list` prints what's left, zone by zone.
- `/ep route` plans the route again from where you stand and prints it. `/ep skip` passes over the current stop. `/ep arrow` shows or hides the arrow.

Waypoints are the game's own map pin, named in WaypointUI when that addon is loaded.

**OpenMap:** works on its stitched map at any zoom. Once a zone fills a good part of the view it counts as that zone's map: full pins for the zone you're in, faint markers for the others. Zoomed further out only the zone you're in shows, as dots. The "Explored 7/9" line shows under the zone name OpenMap puts in the map's hover label.

Pins disappear as the challenge ticks the area. Nothing is tracked by the addon itself; it reads the challenge's own ticks.

## Where the pins sit

Each pin is at the middle of the part of the map that reveals when you explore that area. For most areas walking to the pin is enough. For a few with odd shapes (coasts, caves, city gates) the exact spot that counts can be a short walk from the pin: keep going until the area name pops up on screen.

## Data

`Data.lua` is generated by `tools/build_data.py` from WoW Forever's own game tables (the DB2 exports on wago.tools): the challenges' criteria and the map overlay each one stands for. Where Forever redrew a zone's map to new bounds but kept the old map's positions (Eastern Plaguelands), the script moves them onto the new map using Classic Era's map bounds. After a game patch that adds zones, run:

```
python tools/build_data.py --refresh
```

## Travel planning

Travel between zones is worked out by [FarstriderLib](https://github.com/Deathwing/FarstriderLib) and its data package, by Savi and Deathwing (GPL-3.0), bundled in `Libs/`. Only its WoW Forever files are included. `tools/vendor_farstrider.py` refreshes the bundled copy from an installed one; it changes one line per file so the library keeps its tables apart from Explorer's (the script's header says how). If you also have FarstriderLib installed as an addon, the newest copy is the one that runs.

Flight paths are used only between flight masters your character knows, and the game only says which those are while a flight master's map is open. Open one on each continent once.

## Walking

Walks go by the ground: `Nav.lua` knows steep ground, water, walls and the road network, and takes a straight line where the ground allows it and the roads where it doesn't. The stop order in your zone, the arrow, the line on the map and the walks between zones all use it. 

The data in `Nav/` is built from AzerothGPS's data, (c) AzerothGPS, all rights reserved, used with its author's permission. `tools/build_nav_data.py` rebuilds it from the latest WoW Forever release of AzerothGPS on CurseForge: its terrain grid with its bridges' floors and its caves' and tunnels' floors laid over it (on its own the grid closes those off), and its road network with the course of every road.

AzerothGPS's roads come in dozens of unconnected pieces (it has an off-road router of its own for the gaps). `tools/shape_nav.py` (run by the build) does that part ahead of time, over that ground, into `Nav/Shapes.lua`: walks between roads that pass close to each other, walks that join the pieces, the walk from every explore pin and every flight master to the junctions nearest it, and a walk between pins that are close together with something in the way. Roads are only joined over open ground, or where a road stops and carries on across a short gap. A pin that itself stands on blocked ground crosses as little of it as will do. It takes about ten minutes to run.

`tools/test/sweep.py` checks the result zone by zone: the walk between every two pins of a zone, how much of it is over blocked ground away from the roads, and how it compares with the straight distance. It covers the Eastern Kingdoms and Kalimdor; anywhere else a walk is a straight line.

`Rides.lua` holds, for each faction, the boats and zeppelins only the other one is offered. The bundled FarstriderLib decides by faction when it loads which ones it knows, so `tools/build_rides.py` loads it once as each faction (in Lua 5.1, against the tests' stand-in client) and keeps the difference. Run it again after `tools/vendor_farstrider.py` brings in a new FarstriderLib.

## Tests

`python tools/test/run.py [folder holding lupa]` loads the addon in Lua 5.1 against a stand-in for the game's frame API and runs:

- `smoke.lua`: the addon's own logic (pins, progress, popup, route, arrow, window) in a made-up world, once for each look.
- `factions.lua`: every journey between two zones, once as Alliance and once as Horde, in the real world: the game's map bounds (`world.lua`, written by `make_world.py` from the game's map tables) and the addon loaded whole in its `.toc`'s order, bundled FarstriderLib and all. It checks that no journey rides anything of the other faction's but by the last resort, that the last resort is only taken where there is no other way and with as few of their rides as will do, and that with it off a journey with no way is told as one. `--quick` runs it from five zones only.

## Install

Copy the `Explorer` folder into `World of Warcraft\_forever_\Interface\AddOns`, or use the DrangedAddons manager.
